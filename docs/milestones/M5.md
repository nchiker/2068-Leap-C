## 3. The shell

`rom/exrom_v2_shell.asm` at the V2 reference tag is the source (V2's current release `v2.0.2-emu.1` differs from it, and its editor engine from the vendored one, only in comments: checked). The vendored editor engine `rom/exrom_editor.asm` is used unchanged; the shell is its redraw and navigation hooks and the loop around it. Each routine names its V2 origin in its header.

**Kept from V2** (adapted to Leap-C's cells and line store): the command loop (`V2_COMMAND_LOOP` → `SH_LOOP`), storing a line (`V2_STORE_EDIT`, `V2_TOKENIZE` → `SH_STORE_EDIT`: the verbatim record in `TOKEN_BUF`), navigation with commit (`V2_NAV`, `V2_NAV_COMMIT`), PROGRAM FULL keeping the typed line (V2's D9b), the error-line jump (`V2_JUMP_TO_ERROR_LINE`), the full and selective redraws (`V2_REDRAW`, `V2_REDRAW_ACTIVE`, `V2_REDRAW_NAV`), the status row with V2's colours and swatch, LIST, the command matching, the startup chime (V2 production's chord, `V2_MAINT_CHIME_C`).

**Not carried over** (BASIC only): the tokenizer stage, keyword spans, variable sessions and immediate statements, arrays and the Array Store, packages, SAVE ... ALL, BASIC's message set.

**Changed for C:**

1. **Highlighting** marks keyword characters with bit 7 in the copy of a settled line (`TOKEN_BUF`), and the drawing routine draws them bold. V2 kept up to 8 spans per line and showed its keywords in upper case; C is case-sensitive, so keywords show as typed, and there is no span limit (§7).
2. **The view** (`SH_VIEW`). V2 put up to 20 lines above the cursor's line whatever their height, so with lines that wrap the cursor's line could fall off the screen (V2's BASIC lines rarely wrap; C's often do: 20 two-row lines need 40 rows). Leap-C measures the rows of the lines above and drops lines from the top until the cursor's line fits whole on rows 0-22.
3. **The redraw when the cursor's line changes height** (it starts or stops wrapping): V2 redrew the whole screen; Leap-C redraws from the cursor's line down when the view stays (§6).
4. **Row clearing** (`SH_CLEAR_ROW`, 8 scanlines with `ldir`, about 6 kT): V2 used `GFX_CLEAR_ROW_TEXT`, which prints 32 spaces (about 23 kT). It was the largest part of a keystroke's cost (§6).
5. **RUN** compiles through `SH_COMPILE` and runs through `RT_RUN` (§8).
6. **LIST** starts a new page before a line that does not fit (V2 cut a wrapped line off at the page end).
7. Commands: `RUN`, `LIST`, `NEW` (Phase 6 adds `FREE`, Phase 7 `SAVE`, `LOAD`, `VERIFY`).

## 4. RST `$30` for `LEX_NEXT`

`LEX_NEXT` is the compiler's most frequent call: 60 `call LEX_NEXT` (3 B each) in `decl.asm` (22), `expr.asm` (15), `stmt.asm` (14), `preproc.asm` (5) and `init.asm` (4). Each became `rst $30` (1 B), and the vector holds `jp LEX_NEXT` in the three bytes page zero had reserved for it (`$0030-$0032`, where `di / halt` stood). Code that moved closer let `tools/relax_jumps.py` turn 7 `jp` into `jr`.

| | Bytes |
|---|---:|
| 60 call sites × 2 B | −120 |
| 7 jumps relaxed | −7 |
| the vector (inside page zero's existing range) | 0 |
| **Net HOME** | **−127** |

Cost: 4 T per token (`rst` 11 T + `jp` 10 T against `call` 17 T): about 0.3% of the lexer's 1,529 T per token (M4). Generated code never uses RST `$30`; the optional size template set (Phase 8, `spec.md` §5.6) would reach its `LDL`/`STL` by `call`.

## 5. C punctuation: keys and glyphs

**Key map** (delta D7, the owner-approved baseline of `spec.md` §6.2), in place in `KEY_ASCII_TABLE_SYMBOL`:

| SYMBOL SHIFT + | Y | U | F | G | S | A | H | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | `[` | `]` | `{` | `}` | `\|` | `~` | `^` | `\` |

V2 decoded SYMBOL SHIFT + A and S as `KEY_NEXT_ERROR`/`KEY_PREV_ERROR` before the table; nothing in Leap-C handles those codes (the editor engine passes them to the navigation hook, which ignored them), so the decoding went (−19 B) and A and S reach the table.

**Glyphs** (delta D8), in the letters' columns like the rest of the font, bit 0 clear for the bold renderer:

| `[` | `]` | `{` | `}` | `\|` | `~` | `^` | `\` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `70 40 40 40 40 40 70 00` | `70 10 10 10 10 10 70 00` | `18 20 20 40 20 20 18 00` | `C0 20 20 10 20 20 C0 00` | `20 20 20 20 20 20 20 00` | `00 00 40 A8 10 00 00 00` | `20 50 88 00 00 00 00 00` | `00 40 20 10 08 04 00 00` |

`\` mirrors `/`; `|` is a solid bar (the gap of `!` tells them apart). The punctuation table (`PUNCT_CHAR_TABLE`, scanned linearly for every punctuation character drawn) was reordered by frequency in C (`;`, `(`, `)`, `,`, `=`, `_`, `*`, `{`, `}`, ...); each character keeps its V2 font index.

**Tests:** `keys-c-charset` types all 95 characters of C's basic source set on the matrix (26 + 26 letters, 10 digits, space, the 29 graphic characters) and reads both the edit line and the screen (the editor's word wrap joined back); `keys-new-symbols` checks each new key's code; `glyphs-new` compares the 8 glyphs, as `GFX_PUTCHAR` draws them, with their designed bytes; `glyphs-distinct`: no two printable characters share a glyph, none but space is blank, and no letter's bold glyph equals another character's plain one (so highlighting is unambiguous on the screen).

## 7. Keyword highlighting

The scanner (`SH_KW_MARK`) walks a line once: a run of letters, digits and `_` is a word; one of at most 8 characters is copied to `TOK_BUF` and looked up by `LEX_KEYWORD`, the lexer's own routine and table in HOME. HOME is mapped whatever the paging, and the lookup reads only `TOK_BUF`, `TOK_LEN` and its table, so the shell calls it with the EXROM paged in; the EXROM holds no keyword list, and the highlighted set is exactly the set the compiler recognises (the C89 keywords, the reserved `long`, `float`, `double`, `union`, `volatile` included). String literals and character constants (with `\` escapes), `//` comments and `/* ... */` comments within the line are skipped.

**Fixture** (`highlight-fixture`, read back with bold cells marked; **bold** below):

```
int main(void)                      -> **int** main(**void**)
  unsigned int1; char _if, ifx;     -> **unsigned** int1; **char** _if, ifx;
  puts("int while \" for");         -> (nothing bold)
  c = '"' + 'i';                    -> (nothing bold)
  for (;;) break; // return int     -> **for** (;;) **break**; // return int
  /* if */ do x++; while (x);       -> /* if */ **do** x++; **while** (x);
  /* else */ INT Int;               -> (nothing bold)
  sizeof(long) + sizeof x;          -> **sizeof**(**long**) + **sizeof** x;
  /* int                            -> (nothing bold)
  return 0;                         -> **return** 0;   (and plain while the cursor is on it)
```

**Limitation (documented in `spec.md` §6.1):** each line is scanned on its own, so a line that continues a `/* ... */` comment begun on an earlier line is highlighted as code. Carrying the comment state would need either a scan from the program's start at every redraw or per-row state that the selective redraws cannot keep consistent with the full one.

## 8. RUN and recovery

`RUN` on the append line: `SH_COMPILE` (HOME) pages the EXROM out (depth 1 → 0), calls `CC_PROGRAM`, pages it back; the shell clears the screen and calls `RT_RUN`, which pages the EXROM out for the program and restores everything at its end (`spec.md` §5.7). Then:

| Outcome | Report (status row) | Cursor | Case |
|---|---|---|---|
| ended | none; the program's screen until a key | as before | `editor-run-typed` (prints 49; the banking invariants armed throughout) |
| compile error | `ERROR 20 LINE 3` | the line, its text loaded | `D4-typed-run-path`, `run-error-at-end` |
| no `main` | `ERROR 71 LINE m` (the last line) | that line | `run-no-main` |
| runtime error | `RUNTIME ERROR 4 LINE 1`, over the program's screen | the failing function's line | `run-runtime-error` |
| stack overflow | `RUNTIME ERROR 1 LINE 1`; after an edit a second RUN works | the function's line | `run-stack-overflow` |
| BREAK in a loop, in `beep()`, in `pause()` | `BREAK LINE 1` | the function's line | `run-break-loop` (real CAPS SHIFT + SPACE; invariants armed), `run-break-beep`, `run-break-pause` |

After every outcome the case checks: back at the editor's key wait with the boot's stack pointer (`$FEFC`), paging depth 1, `RUNNING` 0, the AY's volumes 0, the source unchanged. A key left in the latch by the program (BREAK) is discarded before the report waits. `u6-colour-state-restored` (V2's U6): a program's border, colours and `mode(1)` do not reach the editor (normal mode, white border, the editor's attributes), and a second RUN starts with clean colours.

Phase 6 replaces the numbers with `ERROR n: message`, adds the exit status, image size and free heap reports and `FREE`, and runs the corpus through this RUN path.

### 9.2 Harness changes

- `ldir` in `SH_KW_MARK`: BC is the word's length (1-8, checked before), DE `TOK_BUF` (128 B). `SH_CLEAR_ROW`'s `ldir` (31 B per scanline) stays inside the row: HL is the row's first byte of a scanline (`(row & $18) | $40`, `(row & 7) << 5`), and B, C are saved around it.
- Every `djnz` count is set just before its loop (the bar 29, the attributes 32, a row's length + 1 so that an empty row draws nothing, a keyword's length ≥ 2). `jp (hl)` (`SH_JP_HL`) reaches only `GFX_PUTCHAR` or `GFX_PUTCHAR_BOLD`.
- Stack balance: every early `ret` in `SH_LIST` (BREAK) and `SH_REDRAW_NAV` (`.full_pop`) pops what its path pushed; the run cases check that the editor's key wait has the boot's stack pointer after every RUN outcome.
- Carry contracts: the selective redraws return NC only after drawing (`SH_RD_DRAW_ACTIVE` ends with `or a`); `SH_REDRAW_ACTIVE`'s height-change path clears carry itself before its `sbc` (`SH_VIEW` returns with carry set) and after `SH_REDRAW.record`.
- `SH_COMPILE`: depth 1 → 0 around `CC_PROGRAM`, whose errors return through `CC_SP` with carry into it; the flags survive `BANK_PAGE_EXROM_IN` (pushed). The interrupts stay on.
- RST `$30`: a `call` and an `rst` push the same return address; nothing counted call lengths (the build re-assembled every jump; `relax_jumps.py` only shortens jumps that are in range).
- Delta D7: `.sym_table_lookup` had no other referrer. Delta D8: `GFX_CLEAR_ROW_TEXT` had no referrer left (`tools/rom_inventory.py`: unreachable).

## 12. Design decisions and deviations

1. **RST `$30` serves the compiler** (owner decision), not the optional size templates (`spec.md` §2.3, §5.6).
2. **The keyword table is not duplicated in the EXROM:** highlighting calls the lexer's lookup in HOME; the 250 B keyword-table budget becomes 141 B of scanner (L35).
3. **Keywords are shown bold as typed** (V2 showed BASIC's in upper case): C is case-sensitive.
4. **The view keeps the cursor's line whole on the screen**, and a line that changes height redraws only from it down (both improvements over V2, verified by the redraw oracle).
5. **`SH_CLEAR_ROW`** replaces the platform's `GFX_CLEAR_ROW_TEXT`, which was then unreachable and removed (D8).
6. **Phase 5's RUN** reports errors by number (`ERROR n LINE m`); the texts are Phase 6's diagnostics (EXROM budget 650 B, untouched).
7. **Commands are case-insensitive and recognised only alone on the append line** (V2's rule); a C line that is exactly `run`, `list` or `new` cannot be stored from the append line (it is not valid C on its own).
8. **The test harness runs ZEsarUX at its emulator speed setting** (`--emulatorspeed 1000 --frameskip 49`), with identical emulated timing.

## 13. Limitations and remaining risks

- **Comment continuation lines** are highlighted as code (§7).
- **A key that makes the cursor's line change height** costs up to about 0.5 M T (it redraws the lines below; no screen clear). The full redraw, when the view moves, costs about 0.4-0.9 M T, as in V2.
- **BREAK does not stop a compile** (no program runs; BREAK is an ordinary key then). A long compile (about 13 ms per line) cannot be interrupted; Phase 6 measures the corpus's compile times.
- **Exit status, image size and free heap are not shown** after a run until Phase 6.
- **Physical key legends:** the owner-approved mapping uses SYMBOL SHIFT positions that are keyword tokens on the stock ROM (`spec.md` §6.2), so a stock keyboard's printed legends do not show the C characters on all eight keys. Phase 9 checks the mapping on the physical keyboard.
- The HOME reserve is 237 B above its floor; Phase 7's tape block I/O has its own 340 B budget.
